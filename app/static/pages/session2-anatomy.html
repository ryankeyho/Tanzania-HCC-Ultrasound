02The eight segments

Couinaud divides the liver into eight segments, each with its own portal vein, hepatic artery and bile duct branch at its center. The hepatic veins run between segments, in three vertical planes. The portal plane, where the portal vein divides, runs across them.

BoundarySeparates
Right hepatic veinRight posterior (VI, VII) from right anterior (V, VIII)
Middle hepatic vein (Cantlie’s line, IVC to gallbladder fossa)Right liver (V–VIII) from left liver (II–IV)
Left hepatic veinLeft medial (IVa, IVb) from left lateral (II, III)
Portal planeUpper segments (II, IVa, VII, VIII) from lower segments (III, IVb, V, VI)
  • Segment I, the caudate lobe, lies behind the others, bounded in front by the ligamentum venosum. It drains straight into the IVC through its own small veins, which is why it can enlarge when the rest of the liver shrinks, as in cirrhosis.
  • Numbering. The international (Brisbane 2000) terminology now prefers Arabic numerals, 1 to 8.
  • Use the umbilical fissure and the umbilical portion of the left portal vein to distinguish segment IV from II/III. This includes the left hepatic vein (LHV), the falciform ligament and ligamentum teres, and the left portal vein.

03Portal veins or hepatic veins?

Portal veinsHepatic veins
WallBright, echogenicNo visible bright wall
PositionInside segments, running with the artery and bile ductBetween segments
DirectionBranch out from the porta hepatisGrow larger toward the diaphragm and join the IVC

This tells you where you are. Bright-walled vessels converging on a hilum: you are near the porta hepatis. Bare channels converging upward on the IVC: you are near the top of the liver.
