Assessment

Course Completion Criteria

Three requirements. Passing two of them is not passing.

A trainee completes the program on meeting all three

  • Posttest score of 80% or above
  • 10 completed and scored OSCE scans logged
  • Final OSCE total of 28/35 or above, with no single OSAUS domain scored below 3

01OSCE minimum domain scores

The third criterion has two parts, and the second is doing real work. A trainee can reach 28 out of 35 while scoring excellent equipment handling, systematic technique, and thorough documentation, but display limited ability to recognize what is on the screen.

That trainee should not be screening independently, and a total score alone would pass them. This is why we have a minimum score of 3 in every domain: it prevents a strong performance in six areas from outweighing limited ability in a different area. A minimum of 3 in all seven domains has also been used as a competence standard in a registered POCUS training study (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05274581). All thresholds on this page are this program’s own completion rules. They mark completion of training, not credentialing for independent practice: graduates continue with expert over-read and quality review.

02Not meeting a criterion

The point of assessment in a training program is to direct the next stage of training, not to sort people. A trainee short of the scan count needs more OSCE sessions. A trainee failing one OSCE domain needs targeted work on that domain, then reassessment, not a repeat of the whole course. We can provide one-on-one remediation for those who need more time following the OSCE sessions.

03After completion

Completion means ready to start POCUS in appropriate settings, with the caveat that there is access to expert over-read of stored images, a functioning referral pathway, and the option to escalate anything uncertain. It does not mean you are ready to work without those and function as a sole ultrasound screener for lesions. They are part of the model, not training wheels to be removed.

The one criterion worth holding firm on is the interpretation domain. Everything else can be remediated with practice; a scanner who cannot recognize findings is the one whose negative reports carry the most risk.
