Welcome

This course trains clinicians to acquire and document liver ultrasound examinations for HCC surveillance in eligible at-risk patients, recognize suspicious observations and arrange appropriate referral. The goal is not to train or expect you to diagnose on ultrasound, but rather to train you to provide quick point-of-care ultrasound in patients who have a higher pretest probability of having a suspicious lesion.

01Reading and things to complete before Session 1

  • AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance on HCC, the surveillance section. The rules this course uses are summarized in Kamaya et al., Radiology 2024 (free to read).

The Pretest. Thirty-nine questions. You are not expected to know this material yet and a low score costs you nothing, it is a baseline, taken again at the end so the program can measure what the course actually taught. The posttest is taken once at the end of the course. Its answers and explanations appear after you submit it.

The Confidence Survey. Seven questions about how confident you feel, not what you know. Answer honestly in both the pre- and post-course survey, since your answers will be important for revising this course.

02What to expect from the didactic weeks

Each session starts with short videos and readings, then hands-on tasks on the Vscan, then key points. Longer background reading is kept in optional sections. Each session ends with a session quiz you can retake as often as you like, which is for your own knowledge and not for grading, and the rationale for every answer appears once you submit.

One request When something in these pages does not match what you see on a real patient, or what local practice does, say so. This curriculum is new, it was written largely from guidelines produced elsewhere, and the people best placed to catch what does not transfer are the people using it here. Please contact us with anything you find incorrect or confusing.
