Week 2 · Session 6

Structured Reporting & Interpretation Practice

By the end of this session you should be able to

  • Complete a standardized screening report from a scan
  • Triage a case correctly to routine surveillance, short-interval repeat, or referral
  • Write an impression a referring clinician can act on without reading the findings
  • Recognize the report phrasings that cause downstream harm

01The report structure

Negative report example

Indication: HBV cirrhosis, routine 6-month surveillance.
Adequacy: Adequate (VIS-A). Both lobes and hepatic dome visualized.
Parenchyma: Coarse heterogeneous echotexture with nodular surface contour. Portal vein 14 mm with normal hepatopetal flow. Spleen 14 cm. No ascites, no collaterals.
Focal lesion: None identified.
AFP: 7 ng/mL.
Extrahepatic: CBD 5 mm. Pancreas incompletely seen, no mass in the visualized portion. Aorta normal caliber. No abnormal nodes at the porta or celiac axis.
Impression: US-1. Adequate surveillance study (VIS-A). Cirrhotic morphology with splenomegaly; portal vein diameter alone is nonspecific and is not sufficient by itself to diagnose portal hypertension, but is noted alongside the absence of ascites or collaterals. No focal lesion seen in the visualized liver. No significant extrahepatic abnormality.
Next step: Continue 6-month surveillance. Next due [date].

The phrase to avoid "No evidence of malignancy" overstates what a screening ultrasound establishes and invites false reassurance in both the clinician and the patient. Write "no focal lesion seen in the visualized liver" instead.

For case sets 1–3, complete the report form on each before discussing it, describing the parenchyma in your own words rather than reaching for "normal" or "abnormal."

03Referral for further imaging needed?

Five scenarios that cover most of what you will meet. Decide before opening each answer.

CaseCirrhosis. Adequate study. New 0.7 cm hypoechoic nodule, segment VI. AFP 6 ng/mL.
Repeat ultrasound in 3–6 months. Below the size threshold and AFP below the action level. Document the segment and both measurements so the next scan can compare properly.
CaseCirrhosis. Adequate study. 2.1 cm heterogeneous lesion with a thin halo, segment VIII. AFP 11 ng/mL.
Refer for multiphase CT or MRI. Size alone triggers this. The normal AFP does not downgrade it, the two arms of the algorithm operate independently.
CaseCirrhosis. Adequate study, no focal lesion. AFP 240 ng/mL, up from 30 six months ago.
Refer for multiphase CT or MRI. A markedly elevated and rising AFP triggers imaging on its own. Given the known limits of ultrasound sensitivity, the negative scan is not reassuring here.
CaseCirrhosis. Study limited, dome not visualized. No lesion in the visualized liver. AFP 45 ng/mL.
Refer for cross-sectional imaging. Neither element is reassuring: the study could not exclude disease where it is most likely to hide, and the AFP independently meets the recall threshold.
CaseNon-cirrhotic HBV carrier. Adequate study. 1.6 cm lesion previously characterized as a hemangioma on contrast-enhanced CT or MRI, uniformly hyperechoic with posterior enhancement on ultrasound, unchanged over three years.
Document as stable with benign features; continue routine surveillance. Prior characterization as a hemangioma on appropriate diagnostic imaging, combined with three years of documented B-mode stability, does not warrant recall. Note what made this safe: the prior cross-sectional characterization, not the B-mode appearance and stability alone. Without that prior characterization, serial B-mode stability by itself would not be sufficient to call a lesion this size definitely benign in an at-risk liver.

04When you are uncertain

The correct action is neither to report normal nor to report HCC. Store representative images and escalate for expert over-read. In a task-shifted program this is the designed safety net, using it is competent practice, not a failure of it.

A finding you cannot reproduce from a second window should be documented as non-reproducible, with the attempt recorded.

05Counselling the patient

What the patient takes away determines whether they return in six months, which is where most of the program's value sits. The accurate framing is short:

"We did not see any lesion today. This test is good but not perfect, so coming back at the scheduled time is what makes the screening work."

Not "your scan is clear, nothing to worry about." That is the sentence after which people stop attending.
